# audit-loom

The audit Loom is the AKHARA outbound weapon. It is not a demo. It is not a pitch deck. It is a forensic walk through *their* business that ends with one specific fix at one specific price. If the prospect watches more than thirty seconds, the Loom has earned a reply rate north of thirty percent.

This skill produces two artifacts: the **shot list** (what is on screen, second by second) and the **script** (what the Voice agent reads). Both are sized for 90–180 seconds. Never longer.

## Inputs you need before drafting

Refuse to draft until you have these. Ask for the missing ones in a single batched message — never one at a time.

1. **Prospect name** and one-line description (city, segment, scale).
2. **Their primary surface** — website, Google profile, Instagram, AmSpa listing. URL.
3. **The observation** — the specific thing wrong, broken, slow, or absent that you noticed. One sentence.
4. **The cost frame** — how the observation maps to lost revenue or wasted spend. A rough number range is enough.
5. **The fix** — which AKHARA agent or department resolves it. From: architect, closer, operator, strategist, conductor, scout, editor, producer, voice, vault.

If you cannot answer #3 specifically, the Loom is not ready to record. Push back: ask for a specific observation, not a guess.

## The five-beat structure

Every Loom follows the same five beats, in order. Do not reorder. Do not skip.

**Beat 1 — Name them (0:00–0:10).**
Open by addressing the prospect by name and location. Show their surface on screen — the page you are auditing. One sentence: who they are, in one phrase.

> "Brookside Med, three locations in Westchester County. Backed by Trilogy Capital since last March."

**Beat 2 — The observation (0:10–0:30).**
Point at the specific thing. Use your cursor on screen. Be specific to the point of discomfort.

> "I clicked your contact form at 8 PM on Tuesday. It is now Saturday morning. Nobody has replied. I checked your Google profile — your reply rate to reviews is twenty-three percent."

**Beat 3 — The cost (0:30–0:60).**
Quantify. Even a range is enough. Translate the observation into dollars per month.

> "At your AOV and the AmSpa-published recovery rate for missed inbound, this is around fourteen-thousand a month in bookings that are leaving for the next result on the page. Over a year, that is one twenty-eight off your top line."

**Beat 4 — The fix (1:00–1:30).**
Name the AKHARA agent. State what it does. State the speed.

> "The Closer answers every inbound within ninety seconds. Books, qualifies, reschedules. It does the work of a fifteen-person front desk. Live inside seven to fourteen days."

**Beat 5 — The ask (1:30–1:50).**
One specific next step. A calendar link, a reply, a number. Never "let me know if interested."

> "I built you a thirty-second draft of what the Closer would have said on Tuesday at 8 PM. The link is in the description. Reply with `send it` and I will send the full implementation note."

End. No outro. No "thanks for watching."

## Voice direction for the script

Read the script as if you are giving a diagnosis, not a sales pitch. Slow. Concrete. No upward inflection at the end of sentences. Pause at every period — the silence is more compelling than the words.

When delivered via ElevenLabs, set stability around 0.45 and similarity 0.85 for the house voice. Lower stability if the voice is reading numbers and you need variation.

## Shot list conventions

The shot list runs in the left column. Time codes in `MM:SS` format. One line per shot. Examples:

```
00:00–00:08  Cursor lands on prospect homepage. Zoom to logo. Hold.
00:08–00:18  Pan to their "Contact" page. Highlight form. Cursor click.
00:18–00:28  Cut to Gmail inbox, filtered to their domain, empty.
00:28–00:42  Cut to their Google profile. Highlight "23% reply rate" number.
00:42–01:00  Whiteboard: $14K/mo. Annotate × 12 = $168K/yr.
01:00–01:20  Cursor lands on akhara.co/closer. Hold on the stat block.
01:20–01:40  Cut to AKHARA logo. Hold. Cursor moves to comment box.
```

Keep shots over six seconds. Cuts faster than that look frantic and undercut the monastic tone.

## What an audit Loom is not

- Not a demo of AKHARA's tooling. They do not care how it works.
- Not a comparison to competitors. Never name competitors.
- Not a list of all problems. One observation, one cost, one fix. Anything else dilutes the close.
- Not a portfolio reel. Show their site, not yours.
- Not improvised. Always scripted, always edited, always under 180 seconds.

## When to refuse

Refuse to draft the Loom if:
- The observation is generic ("your site is slow"). Specificity is the entire weapon.
- The prospect is below the floor — single-location, under $2M revenue, no signal of professional management. Push back: this is a department offer, not a freelance gig.
- The cost frame cannot be defended with a real number. If you have to guess wildly, the prospect will spot it.

## After the draft

End your output with three follow-up questions for the operator:

1. **Recording length target** — 90s, 120s, or 180s. Defaults to 120.
2. **Voice** — house Voice clone, or read by the operator. Defaults to house Voice.
3. **CTA destination** — calendar link, email reply, or signed pre-proposal. Defaults to a reply with a code word.

## The brutal version

The audit Loom is not creative work. It is forensic work with a script. Find the specific thing they are losing money on. Quantify it. Name the fix. Ask for the small next step.

If you cannot find the specific thing in fifteen minutes of looking at their surface, they are the wrong prospect, not a research problem. Move to the next one on the list.
