# cold-outreach

Cold outreach is a function of three things: the specificity of the observation, the brevity of the ask, and the absence of pitch. Get those right and reply rates land where they should — 8% on cold DMs, 15% on cold emails to a researched ICP, 30%+ with a paired audit Loom.

This skill produces messages that meet that bar. Every other element of "good cold email advice" you have read is filler.

## The three-line template

Every first-touch message is three lines. No exceptions.

**Line 1 — The specific observation.**
Something true about *them* that proves you looked. Not their industry. Not "I love what you're building." A specific surface they own that you noticed something about.

> "Your contact form has been silent since Tuesday — I checked Wednesday, Thursday, this morning."

**Line 2 — The implication.**
What that observation costs them. Numbers if you have them, sized estimates if you don't. No threats, no fear-mongering — just the math.

> "At your AOV and the AmSpa-published recovery rate for unanswered inbound, that's roughly $14K a month leaving for the next result on the page."

**Line 3 — The micro-ask.**
A small, reversible next step. Never "jump on a call." Never "discuss opportunities." Something they can do in 30 seconds.

> "I made a 90-second walkthrough showing the fix. Reply 'send' and I'll drop the link."

That is the entire message. Sign with first name only.

## Subject lines (email only)

Subject lines are nouns, never verbs. No questions. No emojis. No urgency theatre.

**Use:**
- "Brookside Med · contact form"
- "Q3 inbound numbers"
- "The Westchester gap"
- "Saturday morning — your inbox"

**Refuse:**
- "Quick question"
- "Following up"
- "RE: " (when there is no actual prior thread)
- "Saw your post!!"
- Any subject longer than five words.

## Banned openers

Never start a cold message with:

- "Hope you're well" / "Hope this finds you well"
- "I came across your profile"
- "I love what you're building"
- "Congrats on the [Series X / new role / award]"
- "My name is [name] and I'm the [title] at [company]"
- "I'll keep this brief"
- "I know you're busy so I'll get straight to the point"
- "Quick one"

These signal "this is generic, please archive." Open with the observation. Always.

## Banned closes

Never end with:

- "Let me know if you have any questions!"
- "Would love to hop on a call if you're open to it"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- "Have a great rest of your week"
- "Best regards" / "Best" / "Cheers" (sign-offs are okay but use first name only)
- "P.S." (especially "P.S. I noticed your..." — that observation belongs in line 1)
- Calendar links unsolicited

The ask in line 3 is the close. Adding more is dilution.

## Length budget

- **LinkedIn DM:** 300 characters max (one screen on mobile, no "see more" tap).
- **Email:** 60 words max for the body.
- **Cold call voicemail:** 25 seconds max — observation, implication, where to reply.
- **SMS (only if you have explicit consent):** 160 characters max, one message, one ask.

Over budget? Cut, do not split. A two-message LinkedIn DM is two chances to be archived.

## Personalization that is not personalization

Filling in `{First Name}` is not personalization. Mentioning their city is not personalization. Mentioning their industry is not personalization. These are mail-merge.

Real personalization is the observation in line 1 — something *they* did, *they* posted, *they* showed on their surface. If you cannot produce that observation, you are not ready to send.

If your tool requires templating across many prospects, the template is the three-line structure. Line 1 is rewritten per prospect. Lines 2 and 3 may be templated.

## What never to send in the first touch

- A pitch deck. Save it for after the reply.
- A calendar link. Earn it.
- A case study. They didn't ask.
- A list of services. Worst form of "show, don't tell" violation.
- An attachment of any kind. Attachments from strangers go to spam.
- "Let me know if I'm reaching out to the right person." If you're not, you didn't research enough.

## What to send on the second touch (if no reply)

Wait 4 business days. Then one follow-up message. One. Never two.

The follow-up is two lines:

> "Saw the form is still quiet — wanted to make sure my last message wasn't buried. The 90-second walkthrough is here if useful: [link]. — [Name]"

That's it. No "just wanted to follow up." No "circling back." If they don't reply to the second touch, they are not a prospect right now. Move on. Note the account and try again in 90 days.

## On follow-ups beyond the second

Don't. Three or more cold touches without a reply is harassment in slow motion. Cold outreach is not persistence. It is precision. If three accounts ignored you, the message is wrong, not your persistence is wrong.

When a campaign stalls, change the observation, not the volume.

## On the audit Loom pairing

A first-touch message paired with an audit Loom outperforms a first-touch message alone by roughly 3x in reply rate. If the operator has the time, pair every cold touch with a Loom.

The message line 3 becomes: "Built a 90-second walkthrough of the fix. Reply 'send' and I'll drop the link." When they reply, send the Loom. This converts a no-cost-to-them request into engagement.

## Voice calibration

The cold message is the AKHARA voice on its most compressed setting. Short. Specific. Confident. No emoji. No exclamation marks. No "I'd love to..." constructions.

Sign with first name only. Never include a title in the signature. Never include a tagline.

## The brutal version

A great cold message is short, specific to them, ends with a small ask, and goes out in volume to a real ICP list. That is the entire skill. Everything else is variation on the theme. If your messages are getting 0% reply rates, the message isn't long enough or short enough — the observation is generic.

Specificity is the only weapon.
