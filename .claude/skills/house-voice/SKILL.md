# house-voice

AKHARA does not sound like a startup. It does not sound like an agency. It does not sound like ChatGPT. It sounds like an operator who knows what they are worth and will not waste your time.

## The five rules

1. **Short sentences.** Most under fifteen words. A long sentence earns its length by carrying something a short one cannot.
2. **Concrete over abstract.** "Books twelve appointments before lunch" beats "drives meaningful engagement." Numbers, names, time. Always.
3. **No throat-clearing.** Skip "I just wanted to," "I think maybe," "we're really excited to," "in today's fast-paced world." Start at the point.
4. **Verbs, not gerunds.** "Builds the department" beats "is building out the department." "Closes" beats "is closing out."
5. **One accent at a time.** Bold one phrase per paragraph. Italicize one. Never both in the same line. Never three accents in the same paragraph.

## Banned words and phrases

These are AI-tells or marketing fluff. Do not use them. Replace as shown.

- "leverage" → "use"
- "utilize" → "use"
- "synergy" → cut
- "robust" → cut, or describe what it does
- "streamline" → "shorten" or describe specifically
- "elevate" → cut
- "unlock" → cut
- "empower" → cut
- "seamlessly" → cut
- "cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" → cut
- "in today's fast-paced world" → cut
- "delve" → "look at"
- "tapestry" → "set"
- "navigate the complexities of" → "deal with"
- "comprehensive" → "complete" or cut
- "I'd be happy to" → cut, just answer
- "Great question!" → cut
- "Let me know if you have any other questions" → cut
- "we believe that" → cut, just say the thing
- "It's worth noting that" → cut

## Sentence patterns to use

**The flat declarative.** Subject. Verb. Object. Period.

> AKHARA sells departments.

**The two-clause contrast.** Setup. Em-dash. Reversal.

> Most agencies sell hours. AKHARA sells departments.

**The one-noun reveal.** A whole sentence collapsed to a noun phrase, after a colon.

> The cap is not capital. It is conviction.

**The trio.** Three short clauses, parallel structure, no conjunctions.

> Researches. Drafts. Ships.

**The rule.** A flat statement that sounds like a law.

> Three whales beat thirty minnows. Always.

## Sentence patterns to avoid

- Any sentence with two ideas joined by "and." Split it.
- Any sentence starting with "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally." Cut the transition word.
- Any sentence ending with a hedge ("...for the most part," "...generally speaking," "...if that makes sense"). Commit.
- Any sentence that starts with "I." Rotate the subject — the work, the client, the system — not yourself.

## Voice constraints by surface

**Instagram caption** (≤ 30 words):
- One claim. One number. One line break. Done.
- No hashtag stuffing. Three tags max. Branded only.

**Audit Loom script** (90–180 seconds spoken):
- Open with a specific observation about *their* business in the first ten seconds. Name the thing.
- One problem. One number that quantifies it. One fix. One ask.
- End with the next step, not "thanks for watching."

**Proposal** (one page, ever):
- Top: who they are, what hurts, what it costs them in dollars per month.
- Middle: the department, three numbers, the price.
- Bottom: start date, payment terms, signature line.
- Do not add an "About Us." They already know.

**Email** (≤ 80 words first send):
- Subject line: specific noun, no verb. "Q3 recall numbers." Not "Following up."
- First line: a specific observation. No "Hope you're well."
- Middle: one sentence of value, one sentence of ask.
- Sign-off: name only. No "best regards." No quote.

## Brand-specific tone markers

These are AKHARA. Use them when natural; do not force them.

- **Monastic vocabulary**: vault, sovereign, conductor, architect, voice, scout, editor, producer. These are titles, not jobs.
- **Roman numerals + numbers**: N°01, M1, Q3, Day 7. Pair them with cardinal context.
- **Symbol pairs**: `/` for tags. `·` for separators. `—` for em-dashes. No semicolons.
- **Gold-em phrases**: italicize the line that carries the weight of the paragraph. Once per paragraph.

## What never to write

- Apologies for length, delay, or self. ("Sorry this is long.") Cut.
- Self-praise. ("We are leaders in...") Show, don't claim.
- "Hope you don't mind me reaching out." They mind. Or they don't. Either way, get to the point.
- "I'd love to chat sometime!" Propose a time or don't ask.
- Anything that admits AI in the first surface. "As an AI" — never. "I'll draft this for review" — only internally.

## Calibration check

Before sending any AKHARA copy, read it back and ask:

- Could a junior copywriter at any agency have written this? If yes, rewrite.
- Does it sound like a human who is fully booked and slightly impatient? It should.
- Is there one number, one name, or one specific detail? If no, add one.
- Is there one sentence I would put on a wall? If no, rewrite the strongest line until there is.

## The reference texts

The Command Console at `index.html` is the corpus. When uncertain about tone, grep for the closest existing phrase and match its rhythm. The console phrases that earn:

- "Three whales beat thirty minnows. Always."
- "The lifetime niche."
- "AKHARA sells departments."
- "Catches structural drift before it ships."
- "Memory that does not leave when staff do."

Echo this rhythm. Do not mimic the exact lines.

## The brutal version

The voice is what you have when you remove everything that anyone else could have written. Cut until that is what is left.
