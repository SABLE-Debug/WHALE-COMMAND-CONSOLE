# ppl-debrief

PPL school is the spine of the operator's daily practice. Most students do the sessions and never compound. The compound comes from the debrief — naming what was tested, what was missed, and what specifically to drill before the next session.

This skill enforces the structure. It does not summarize the session. It produces the next step.

## The debrief shape

```
PPL DEBRIEF — [date · session type · module]

1. What was the session about (one sentence):
   [The single concept or skill that was the focus]

2. What I got right:
   [2–3 specific things you did/identified well. Not vague — specific.]

3. What I missed:
   [2–3 specific things you didn't see, or saw wrong. Specific.]

4. The pattern in my misses:
   [If the misses share a structure, name it. If not, say "no pattern yet."]

5. Coach's feedback (if a coached session):
   [Verbatim or paraphrased, one line per note.]

6. The drill for the next session:
   [The single most leveraged thing to practice. Specific. Measurable.]

7. Open question I need answered:
   [The one thing that's unclear. To ask the coach or research.]
```

If section 6 is empty, the debrief is not done. The drill is the deliverable.

## On "what I got right"

The temptation is to skip this or to fill it with platitudes. Resist both.

Good entries:
- "Correctly identified the H4 structure shift before the live commentary mentioned it."
- "Sized 0.25% on the second entry even though conviction was high — didn't size up."

Bad entries:
- "I was focused."
- "I learned a lot."

The "right" list is the evidence of compound. Without it, you can't tell if you're improving.

## On "what I missed"

Be specific to the point of discomfort. Generic misses do not produce drills.

Good entries:
- "Missed the FVG on the M15 between 09:14 and 09:18 SAST. It was clearly drawn 12 minutes later."
- "Marked the wrong POI on the H1 — used the swing high instead of the order block."

Bad entries:
- "Wasn't paying attention."
- "Got confused."

If you can't articulate the miss, you can't drill it. Push back on yourself: what specifically did you not see?

## On the pattern

The pattern across misses is the single highest-leverage observation in the debrief.

Examples of real patterns:
- "Always missing the second confirmation. Acting on the first signal."
- "Forgetting to check the H4 before drawing the H1 levels."
- "Pulling the trigger before the candle closes."

If you see the same pattern three debriefs in a row, the curriculum is not the bottleneck. The discipline is. The drill becomes a behavior change, not a knowledge add.

## On the drill

A drill is:

- **Specific.** Not "practice patience." "Replay 10 H1 charts from last week and identify only the trades that had H4 alignment, with no other rules."
- **Time-bounded.** "30 minutes a day for the next 3 days."
- **Measurable.** "Identify the structure shift within 60 seconds, accuracy >70%."
- **Repeatable.** Same exercise, repeatable until the skill compounds.

A vague drill ("review the module") is not a drill — it's homework deferral. Push for specificity.

## On the coach's feedback

If the session was live with a coach, capture the feedback verbatim or close to it. Coaches often say the same thing 4 sessions in a row before the student hears it. The journal is how you finally hear it.

If the coach gave 5+ notes, pick the 2 most important. Five drills is no drill.

## On the open question

The one thing that's unclear. Examples:

- "Why does the coach call this a 'continuation' when the structure is technically a reversal?"
- "What's the difference between 'liquidity grab' and 'stop hunt' in this framework?"
- "When the H1 and H4 disagree, which wins?"

These get asked at the next live session, or researched between. Open questions are the markers of where the curriculum and your understanding diverge — the divergence is where the learning happens.

## Weekly aggregation

After a week of debriefs, aggregate:

- **Sessions completed:** [N / scheduled]
- **Recurring misses:** [list the misses that appeared 2+ times]
- **Drills completed:** [N / drills assigned]
- **Open questions answered vs. still open:** [N / N]
- **Net skill movement:** [up / flat / down — honest assessment]

If "flat" or "down" for two consecutive weeks, the practice plan is broken. Possible causes:
- Drills not actually practiced (most common).
- Curriculum mismatch — content above current skill level.
- Energy/time constraints — practicing while exhausted.

The fix is structural, not effort-based. Slow down. Half-sized drills, fully done.

## On the PPL + AKHARA + Prop frame

The console targets 30–60 min/day of trading practice by month 6. The PPL debrief is the highest-leverage part of that time. 15 minutes of debrief produces the next session's drill — that drill is what compounds, not the live session.

If the debrief is being skipped to spend more time on live sessions, the trade is wrong. The debrief is the multiplier.

## Refusals

Refuse to write a debrief that:

- Has no specific misses (every session has misses; "I got everything" is not a debrief).
- Has no drill in section 6.
- Reports a "great session" without naming what specifically was great.
- Is being written as a feel-good entry after a losing week. The losing week needs a harder debrief, not a softer one.

Push back: "What specifically did you miss? What does the next drill look like? If you can't answer those, the debrief isn't done."

## The brutal version

Most students of a paid curriculum get less than 10% of the value because they show up to the live sessions and skip the work between. The debrief is the work between. Forty hours of curriculum without debriefs equals two hours of curriculum with them.

The drill is the deliverable. Everything else is theater.
