# pricing-floor

AKHARA does not sell hours. AKHARA does not sell software. AKHARA sells departments. The price reflects what a department replaces — not what it costs to produce. The floor exists because under-pricing breaks the model, attracts the wrong clients, and signals that you are a freelancer in a suit.

This skill catches and corrects pricing slippage before it leaves your fingers.

## The published floors

These are floors, not list prices. The starting line. Raise above for complexity, never quote below.

**Single department.**
- Setup: $12,000 minimum (single integration), $25,000 ceiling (multi-system).
- Operating: $8,000/mo minimum, $15,000/mo ceiling.

**Department+ (single department + The Conductor).**
- Setup: $20,000 minimum, $35,000 ceiling.
- Operating: $14,000/mo minimum, $22,000/mo ceiling.

**Sovereign (3+ departments).**
- Setup: $40,000 minimum, $80,000 ceiling.
- Operating: $25,000/mo minimum, $45,000/mo ceiling.

## Hard refusals

Refuse — every time — to:

1. **Quote in hourly or daily rates.** "What's your hourly?" gets the answer "AKHARA does not bill by the hour." Then return to scope.
2. **Quote for a single deliverable as a project.** "Build me a workflow" is not a department. Decline and re-frame, or refer them to a freelance marketplace.
3. **Quote a "starter" or "lite" tier below the floor.** The mid-market is below the floor. Operators below the floor are tier 5 — refer out.
4. **Run a free trial or pilot below the floor.** A pilot is a discount on the first month, not a free month. If they need free, they are tier 5.
5. **Drop the setup fee.** The setup fee covers the embedding, integration, and break-in. Without it, you absorb that work into operating, which makes month-one unprofitable and month-six over-priced.
6. **Bundle additional departments without raising the operating fee proportionally.** Each department is an operating cost — model usage, configuration time, monitoring. Bundles do not get free.
7. **Quote in equity, revenue share, or "let's see how it goes."** Cash. Up front. No exceptions.

## Discount logic (the only allowed discounts)

There are exactly three legal discounts. Apply at most one per deal.

**Multi-department discount.** Bundling three or more departments at signing earns 10% off the *operating* fee (never the setup). Stacking with anything else: not allowed.

**Annual prepay discount.** Paying 12 months in advance earns 15% off the operating fee for that 12 months. Refundable pro-rata if AKHARA cancels; not refundable if the client cancels.

**Tier 1 strategic discount.** For a PE-backed rollup that signs a Sovereign deal with a public commitment (case study, intro to portfolio companies, joint announcement), 10% off setup AND operating, for the first 12 months. Reverts to list price at month 13.

Discounts beyond this list require an explicit operator override in writing. Refuse them by default.

## Tells that the user is about to under-price

When you see these patterns in the user's message, slow down and apply the floor:

- "What's the smallest amount you'd do this for?" — Wrong question. Re-frame to scope, not price.
- "They said their budget is $3K/month, can we make it work?" — That budget is below the floor. They are not the customer.
- "Let me just do this one for cheap to get the case study." — Case studies are paid for. Below-floor work does not produce above-floor referrals.
- "What if I just do a one-time project for them?" — One-time projects are freelance work. AKHARA does not do freelance.
- "Can I throw in [extra department] for free?" — No. Extra work earns extra fees.
- "I'll quote it low and we can raise the price later." — You will not raise the price later. You will under-quote forever.

## On "we can't afford that"

The honest response, in AKHARA voice:

> "Understood. AKHARA is built for accounts where the department replaces 15 to 65 hires. At your scale, the math does not yet pencil. Come back when you are running multi-location or above $5M revenue, and we will be a fit. In the meantime, here are three lower-cost options that can hold the line: [name two reputable lower-cost services or referrals]."

No apologies. No discount offers. No "well maybe we could...". This is not a stage of negotiation. It is a tier filter.

## On "your competitor quoted half"

The honest response:

> "Their offer is for a different thing. AKHARA's model replaces departments, not deliverables. If the competitor's offer fits, take it. We're a fit when the failure mode of the cheaper option is more expensive than the price gap."

Do not match. Do not undercut. Do not explain at length. The frame is "different things at different prices, pick the one that fits." End.

## On scope-creep mid-engagement

If a client asks for something beyond the named department, the response is a change order, not a favor:

> "That sits outside The Closer's scope. We can add it via change order — typically $X for setup, $Y/mo. Want me to spec it?"

Change orders that arrive in the first 30 days of a new department are common and expected. They are not free. Capture them.

## Refresh cadence

Review and adjust the floor every six months. Inputs:
- Inflation (CPI for services in your geography).
- Your own utilization (if you are at 90%+ for a quarter, raise the floor).
- The market for the departments you replace (front desk wages, marketing salaries, ops manager comp). Your price floor should remain a fraction of the replaced labor cost.

When the floor changes, the change applies to *new* deals only. Existing deals continue at signed price until renewal.

## The brutal version

Under-pricing is the cheapest form of self-sabotage. It guarantees you work harder for less, attract clients who treat you as expendable, and burn the runway you need to build the thing that actually scales. The floor is not greed. It is the model. Hold it.
