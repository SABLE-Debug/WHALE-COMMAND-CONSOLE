# proposal

AKHARA proposals are one page. Always. A two-page proposal is a tell — it means the operator was not confident enough to compress. The one-page constraint forces brutality. The brutality is the close.

## Structure (top to bottom, in this exact order)

**Header.** Their company name and city. Today's date. A reference number in the form `AKH-N°XX-YYYY`. Nothing else.

**Block 1 — Their context (one paragraph, 3–5 sentences).**
Restate what is true about their business that brought us here. Specific. Numbers if you have them.

> "Brookside Med operates three locations in Westchester County, backed by Trilogy Capital since March. Current inbound volume runs around 1,400 leads per quarter across the three sites, with a published reply rate to inbound web forms of under twenty percent. At your published AOV, this is approximately $14,000 per month leaving for the next result on the page."

**Block 2 — The diagnosis (one sentence).**
One sentence. Italicized. The thing being solved.

> *The cost is not capacity. The cost is the inbound response gap.*

**Block 3 — The department (4–6 sentences).**
Name the AKHARA agent. State what it does. State the operating model. State the speed to live. Three numbers maximum.

> "Deploy The Closer, AKHARA's inbound response department. Answers every inbound web form, call, and DM within ninety seconds, twenty-four hours a day, in your house voice and within your booking system. Books, qualifies, reschedules, never sleeps. Live across all three locations inside fourteen days. Operates at the work volume of a 15-person front desk team at a fraction of headcount cost."

**Block 4 — The price (table, fixed shape).**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| The Closer · setup | $XX,XXX one-time |
| The Closer · operating | $XX,XXX / month |
| Term | 6 months minimum, month-to-month thereafter |
| Cancellation | 30 days written notice |

**Block 5 — The numbers (three only).**
Three projected outcomes. Conservative. Defensible.

> "Twelve-month projection, conservative: $84,000 in recovered booking value at month three. $168,000 by month six. Cumulative twelve-month payback: 4.2x setup, 9x operating."

**Block 6 — Start date and signature.**
Proposed start date (next Monday, or the closest business day a week out). Signature lines for them and you. Wire instructions or PayPal invoice reference. Done.

That is the entire document. Anything else is filler. Cut it.

## Pricing floor (non-negotiable defaults)

Default tier pricing. Discounts allowed only for tier 1 (PE-backed rollups, multi-location, multi-department deal).

**Single department (Closer, Operator, Strategist, Scout, Editor, Producer, Voice, Vault):**
- Setup: $12,000–$25,000 depending on integration count.
- Operating: $8,000–$15,000 per month.

**Department+ (single department + The Conductor):**
- Setup: $20,000–$35,000.
- Operating: $14,000–$22,000 per month.

**Sovereign (any 3+ departments, 60+ day onboarding):**
- Setup: $40,000–$80,000.
- Operating: $25,000–$45,000 per month.

These are floors. Never quote below the bottom of the band without the operator's written agreement.

## What never to write

- **"About Us"** — they already booked the call.
- **A portfolio or case study list** — show outcomes by reputation, not in the doc.
- **"Why AKHARA"** — if they need that pitch, they are below the floor.
- **A team org chart or bios** — irrelevant.
- **A timeline with weekly milestones** — keep it to "live in N days."
- **Tiered options ($5K / $10K / $20K menu)** — propose one solution. Force the binary.
- **"Custom" or "tailored" or "bespoke"** — every proposal is custom. Saying so is empty.
- **Logos of past clients** — vanity.
- **Anything past one page printed** — if it doesn't fit, cut.

## What never to promise

- **Specific revenue numbers in copy that could be construed as a guarantee.** Always say "projected" and "conservative" and base the math on their published numbers. Never promise.
- **Free trials.** A trial is a discount. If they need a trial, they are tier 5.
- **Custom development beyond the named department.** Scope creep dies at the proposal.
- **A start date inside this week.** Always one week out minimum. Discipline.

## On objections that arrive after the proposal

The proposal is the close. Objections after it are bargaining, not buying signals. Common ones, with the standard response:

- *"Can you do it cheaper?"* — No. The price reflects the work. Adjust scope, not price.
- *"Can you start sooner?"* — No. The start date is the start date. Adjust the start date only for tier 1 with reason.
- *"Can we try one month first?"* — No. Six-month minimum. The first month is integration and break-in, not steady state.
- *"Can we revisit in Q4?"* — Yes, but the price floor will be 10% higher. Inflation clause is real.

If they push past three objections, they are not qualified. End the conversation. Save the cycles.

## Drafting protocol

When invoked:

1. Confirm tier and price band before drafting. Refuse if below floor.
2. Pull their context numbers from their published sources or from the audit Loom dossier.
3. Draft on one page. Show me the word count. If over 300 words, cut.
4. Read it once aloud. Cut any sentence that sounds like marketing.
5. Return the proposal as markdown. Do not export. Operator sends from their own client.

## On legal language

Keep it to one line per item: term, cancellation, payment.

Anything beyond that goes into a separate Master Services Agreement that gets signed *with* the proposal. Do not bury legalese inside the proposal body — it dilutes the close.

## The brutal version

The proposal is not a sales tool. It is a confirmation document. By the time the proposal arrives, the audit Loom and the call have already done the selling. The proposal exists to close the loop, lock the price, set the date, and start the work.

If the proposal needs to "sell" the deal, the deal was not ready. Go back to the Loom.
